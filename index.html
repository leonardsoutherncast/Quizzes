1
The Mold — Cope & Drag / The container that shapes the casting
Flask
The rigid frame or box that holds the sand mold together during molding and pouring. It does not become part of the casting — it just contains the sand.
💡 Think of the flask as a picture frame: it holds everything in shape but isn't part of the picture.
Cope
The top half of the flask (and the sand mold inside it). "Cope" always refers to the upper portion.
💡 Memory trick: Cope = Ceiling (top).
Drag
The bottom half of the flask (and the sand mold inside it). The drag sits on the molding floor and supports the cope on top of it.
💡 Memory trick: Drag = Down (bottom).
Parting Line
The flat (or contoured) surface where the cope and drag meet and separate. It divides the mold into its two halves and is a critical reference for pattern design.
💡 The parting line is the "seam" of the mold — you'll often see it as a faint line on the finished casting.
Mold Cavity
The hollow space inside the assembled mold (cope + drag) that is shaped exactly like the desired casting. Molten metal fills this cavity to form the part.
💡 The mold cavity is the "negative" of your finished part — the empty space that metal will occupy.
2
Patterns / The tool used to create the mold cavity
Pattern
A replica of the final casting — usually made from wood, plastic, or metal — that is pressed into sand to create the mold cavity. The pattern is removed before pouring.
💡 The pattern is like a cookie cutter: press it in, pull it out, and the shape remains in the sand.
Draft
A slight taper (angle) added to the vertical walls of a pattern so it can be withdrawn from the sand cleanly without breaking the mold. Measured in degrees.
💡 No draft = the pattern rips the sand when pulled out. Even 1–3° makes a huge difference.
Shrinkage Allowance
The amount added to a pattern's dimensions to compensate for the metal shrinking as it cools from liquid to solid. Patterns are intentionally made larger than the final part.
💡 Metal is like a sponge drying out — it shrinks. The pattern must be oversized to hit the right final dimensions.
Core Print
An extension on the pattern (and corresponding recess in the mold) that locates and supports a sand core inside the mold cavity. Core prints hold cores in position during pouring.
💡 Think of the core print as a "socket" — it's where the core plug sits to stay put.
3
Cores / Creating internal cavities in the casting
Core
A preformed sand shape placed inside the mold cavity to create internal hollow sections — holes, passages, or recesses — in the finished casting that the pattern alone cannot form.
💡 If you want a pipe with a hollow center, a core is what creates that hollow space inside the casting.
Core Box
The tooling (like a mold for cores) used to form the sand core into the correct shape. Sand is packed into the core box, then the box is opened and the core is removed.
💡 The core box is to cores what the pattern is to the mold — it's the tool that shapes them.
Core Coating
A refractory (heat-resistant) liquid applied to the surface of a core before it is placed in the mold. It prevents the hot metal from penetrating the sand, improving the casting's surface finish.
💡 Core coating is like sunscreen for the sand core — it protects it from the extreme heat of molten metal.
4
Gating System / The channel network that delivers metal to the mold
Sprue
The vertical channel through which molten metal first enters the mold from the top. The sprue connects the pouring cup (where the ladle pours) down into the runner system.
💡 The sprue is the "downspout" — metal falls straight down through it to reach the horizontal channels below.
Runner
The horizontal channel at the parting line that distributes molten metal from the base of the sprue outward to one or more gates. Runners control flow speed and direction.
💡 Think of the runner as a hallway — metal runs horizontally through it to reach each doorway (gate) into the mold cavity.
Gate / In-Gate
The final opening where molten metal enters the mold cavity from the runner. The size and shape of the gate control the rate and direction of metal flow into the part.
💡 The gate is the last checkpoint before metal enters the actual casting shape — it's where flow is precisely controlled.
5
Risers, Chills & Chaplets / Controlling solidification and defects
Riser / Feeder
A reservoir of extra molten metal attached to the mold cavity. As the casting solidifies and shrinks, the riser feeds liquid metal into the casting to fill shrinkage voids and prevent internal defects.
💡 The riser is the casting's backup fuel tank — it donates metal to the part as it shrinks during cooling.
Chill
A metal insert (iron or steel) placed in the mold against the sand surface to rapidly extract heat from a specific area, causing that section of the casting to solidify faster than the surrounding metal.
💡 Chills are like ice packs on a sore spot — they cool one area quickly to control how the metal solidifies.
Chaplet
A small metal support piece placed inside the mold cavity to support a core that might sag or shift under the weight of the incoming molten metal. Chaplets become part of the casting.
💡 Chaplets are tiny metal stilts that hold the core in place — they fuse into the casting during pouring.
Mold Coating / Wash
A refractory liquid applied to the mold cavity surfaces before pouring. It creates a barrier between the molten metal and the sand, reducing sand burn-on and improving the casting's surface finish.
💡 Mold wash is like non-stick cooking spray for the mold — it keeps metal from sticking to the sand surface.
6
Casting Process Terms / Key concepts for understanding the process
Green Sand
The most common foundry molding sand — a mixture of silica sand, clay (bentonite), and water. "Green" does not refer to color; it means the sand is used in its moist, unbaked state.
💡 "Green" here means uncured, not the color. Most of Southern Cast's molds are made with green sand.
Shrinkage
The reduction in volume that occurs when molten metal transitions from liquid to solid as it cools. Shrinkage is predictable for each alloy and must be accounted for in pattern design.
💡 Every metal shrinks a specific amount — for example, gray iron shrinks about 1%, while steel can shrink 2–3%.
Porosity
Small voids, holes, or pores inside a casting caused by trapped gas or solidification shrinkage. Porosity weakens the casting and can cause leaks in pressure-tight applications.
💡 Porosity looks like Swiss cheese on the inside — it's a major quality defect that risers and proper venting help prevent.
Solidification
The process by which molten metal transitions from liquid to solid state as it loses heat inside the mold. Controlling solidification direction and rate is key to producing a sound, defect-free casting.
💡 Ideal solidification progresses from the thinnest sections toward the riser — pulling metal in to fill any shrinkage as it goes.
